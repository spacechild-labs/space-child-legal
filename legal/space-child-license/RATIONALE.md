# Space Child License — Design Rationale

## What Makes It Different

### vs. MIT / Apache / GPL (Traditional Open Source)
Traditional licenses are use-agnostic. MIT doesn't care if you build a school or a drone strike. The Space Child License does.

### vs. Hippocratic License 3.0
HL3 prohibits a broad list of human rights violations (slavery, torture, discrimination, ecocide, etc.) but treats all violations equally and has no commercial path for military use. It's essentially a prohibition list.

The Space Child License:
- Is **peace-positive** not just harm-negative — it *encourages* humanitarian use
- Accepts military reality — defensive use is free, offensive use pays
- Creates a **revenue-for-peace** mechanism (25% of military licenses to peace orgs)
- Has a narrower, more enforceable set of prohibitions focused on armed aggression and autonomous killing

### vs. Non-Violent Public License (NPL/CNPL)
NPL prohibits violence broadly but doesn't distinguish defensive from offensive. The cooperative variant restricts commercial use to worker-owned orgs.

Space Child License:
- Allows all commercial use (doesn't restrict capitalism, restricts war)
- Makes the defensive/offensive distinction explicitly
- More commercially viable for adoption

### vs. Do No Harm License
Similar spirit but less structured. Space Child License has formal legal sections, governing law, enforcement mechanisms, and the novel paid-military-license construct.

## The Key Innovation: War Pays

No existing license creates a **paid licensing tier for offensive military use** that funnels revenue to peace organizations. This is novel. It says:

> "We can't stop you from using this for war. But if you do, you're funding peace."

This is pragmatic, not naive. It accepts that governments will do what governments do, but creates an economic mechanism that makes war-use financially contribute to its own opposition.

## Enforceability Considerations

### Strengths
- Built on copyright law (well-established, international treaties like Berne Convention)
- Specific, defined terms (not vague aspirations)
- Governing law specified (Iowa)
- Cure period allows good-faith correction
- Severability clause protects against partial invalidation

### Potential Challenges
- **Use restrictions in copyright licenses are untested** — no court has definitively ruled whether a copyright license can restrict *how* software is used (copyright traditionally controls copying, not use)
- **Patent vs. copyright** — patent licenses can restrict use more naturally; copyright licenses traditionally can't
- **International enforcement** — Iowa governing law may be difficult to enforce against foreign governments
- **Definition of "primarily defensive"** — gray areas exist in military classification

### Mitigation Strategies
- The license grant is conditional (Section 2 says "provided that such use constitutes a Peaceful Purpose") — this is a condition of the grant, not a use restriction, making it more defensible under copyright law
- Automatic termination (Section 6.1) operates through copyright: violating use = no license = copyright infringement
- Patent grant (Section 2.2) adds a second enforcement mechanism
- International human rights framework (Section 8.6) provides interpretive foundation

### What a Lawyer Should Review
1. Whether conditional grants based on use purpose are enforceable under US copyright law
2. Whether the Peaceful Purpose definition is sufficiently clear to survive a vagueness challenge
3. Whether Section 5 (Offensive Military Licensing) creates an enforceable obligation vs. an aspirational framework
4. Whether Section 6.4 (third-party standing) is viable under contract/license law
5. State-specific considerations for Iowa governing law
6. International enforceability, particularly re: sovereign immunity for government licensees

## OSI Compatibility

The Space Child License is **intentionally not OSI-compatible**. The Open Source Initiative defines "open source" as use-agnostic (OSD clause 5: "No Discrimination Against Fields of Endeavor" and clause 6: "No Discrimination Against Groups"). 

We respectfully disagree with the premise that technology must be morally neutral. Technology is never neutral — it amplifies the intentions of its users. A license that acknowledges this isn't less open; it's more honest.

The Space Child License is **source-available, peace-conditional, copyleft for the peace clause**. It's a new category.

## Application Across Space Child Ecosystem

Once finalized and reviewed by counsel, this license would apply to:
- All ~30 repositories in the Space Child ecosystem
- ghostOS, SingularisPrime, cosmic-empathy-core, kannaka-memory, etc.
- Music catalog (with appropriate creative work adaptations)
- Future projects

The license creates a unified legal identity for the ecosystem: **free for peace, accountable for war**.
